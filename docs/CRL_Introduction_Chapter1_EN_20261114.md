# Introduction (Chapter 1, formal English)

Working title: *Identifiability Boundaries and Conditional Recovery*
Date: 2026-11-14. Status: formal manuscript section. Every conditional qualifier is retained.

---

In causal representation learning, questions about the environment — what it is, whether it can be recovered without labels — arise early rather than late. They do not decide the truth of the work built above them, but they can change how that work should be read. A downstream predictor, invariant estimator, or causal claim is defined against a partition, and a partition that is wrong or merely assumed can weaken the analysis that uses it, though rarely in a way that is easy to see from the result alone. Setting the problem out clearly has a narrower payoff: it helps mark which downstream uses are sensitive to the partition and which are less so. This paper asks whether a covariance-based environment structure stays identifiable once the labels are removed and the observations are pooled.

Most multi-environment theory takes the partition as given. The index set of every environment is assumed known, and assumed unique, so identification and estimation can proceed with the assignment held fixed. The convention is convenient and often justified in designed experiments, where interventions are logged and the source of each observation is recorded. It becomes shaky once the labels disappear. A pooled marginal, written $P_X=\sum_e\pi_e G_e$, carries no mark of the component behind any given draw, and the decomposition that the known-index theory starts from is precisely what the analyst now has to reconstruct. Treating that starting point as free is the problem this paper interrogates: with only $P_X$ in hand, neither the assignment nor the number of components is given, and the uniqueness clause in the standard formulations fails at the population level before finite-sample error is even introduced.

Tracing the problem this way brings two questions to the surface, and the paper is built around answering them. (1) Once the labels are removed and only a pooled distribution remains, is the covariance-based environment structure identifiable at all, and where does the boundary between non-identifiability and point identification actually lie, and what can be said when the assumptions are relaxed? (2) When the structure is identifiable in principle, can it be recovered from recording-level covariance without labels, and which design conditions decide whether blind recovery succeeds or fails? The first is theoretical and receives two formal results, a non-identifiability witness and a point-identification proposition, with the relaxed-assumption case discussed rather than presented as a third identification result. The second is methodological and empirical, and it is answered by a recovery procedure whose guarantees are kept separate from the variants we actually run, followed by a pre-specified protocol over three independent EEG datasets.

The relevant literature separates into four groups, and the distinctions are developed in full in Section 2. The first group anchors representation learning through independence, temporal dependence, or interventions and identifies latent components while the environment index is treated as available~\cite{scholkopf2021,comon1994,hyvarinen2017,ahuja2023,squires2023,varici2025}; the index is the object this paper treats as missing, so those guarantees do not transfer. The second group studies mixture identifiability and the moment-order threshold, from full-distribution identifiability of the component parameters to the generic fourth-order non-injectivity and sixth-order recovery of Gaussian mixtures~\cite{teicher1963,yakowitz1968,blomenhofer2023}; these statements concern unordered components, not the partition of observations. The third group covers environment discovery, clustering consistency, and the covariance geometry~\cite{yao2025,murata2025,ng2025,pollard1981,lloyd1982,arsigny2007,pennec2006}; it supplies the clustering machinery and the log-Euclidean coordinate system but stays silent on whether a population optimum aligns with the true environment. The fourth group adopts the partial-identification vocabulary and the unlabeled-mixture line~\cite{manski2003,kanamori2026,chen2026}, targeting different objects from the covariance partition here. None of these constructs the pooled-marginal two-partition witness, and none addresses recording-level covariance recovery under an explicit compatibility condition; that boundary is the gap this paper fills.

The answer is not one verdict but several, and the distinctions between them are the substance of the paper. In a concrete $d=2$ construction, two genuinely different partitions induce the same pooled distribution; a measure-preserving rotation connects them, they disagree on half the support, and no label permutation maps one to the other. This is a single witness, not a general theorem, but it settles that identifiability cannot be granted for free. Tighten the setting to a constrained, whitened two-component frame and the component covariance pair becomes point-identified, with every assumption stated where it is used; we treat that result as a constrained special case rather than a generic fourth-order statement. Relax the assumptions and a single value is no longer determined, so the target can only be described as a set of structures consistent with the pooled data. We discuss this partial-identification region without claiming a sharp set, a set-valued estimator, or a coverage guarantee, and the resampling that accompanies it is a finite-sample diagnostic rather than a coverage theorem.

On the practical side sits DELVE, our recording-level procedure. Under a fixed number of environments and a fixed feature transformation, the global empirical $K$-means solution carries a finite-sample risk-gap guarantee, where the population alignment enters as an explicit compatibility premise that is stated rather than derived. The global, finite-Lloyd, and blind variants are kept deliberately apart; automatic $K$ selection, full-pool standardization, and finite-initialization Lloyd fall outside the unconditional guarantees. The empirical work runs on three independent EEG datasets and asks where each prediction holds and where it breaks down. On the balanced three-source design, fixing $K$ at its oracle value lets covariance features recover source-associated structure with recording-level ARI $0.608$. The pre-specified blind selector returns $K=2$ and reaches $0.279$, merging the same source pair on every seed; the unbalanced recording pool gives $0.371$, and the all-subject plug-in gives $0.241$. We report these as primary findings. The real-data label mixes device, task, population, duration, and preprocessing; it is never a biological or causal environment, and every real-data conclusion is bounded to dataset-level, source-associated structure.

The standard components carry no novelty. Shrinkage covariance estimation, the log-Euclidean feature, nearest-centroid clustering, and the resampling diagnostics are established tools, and each is cited for the exact construction used rather than repackaged as an invention. Our main contributions are:

1. **An identifiability boundary for covariance-based environments: two formal results and a discussion.** Under pooled observations with no environment labels, we give a $d=2$ impossibility witness in which two genuinely different partitions induce the same pooled distribution, and a constrained point-identification result for the component covariance pair in a whitened two-component frame. The relaxed-assumption case is discussed as a partial-identification region but not claimed as a sharp set, a set-valued estimator, or a coverage result. The two formal statements carry distinct assumptions, and neither is presented as a generic fourth-order result.

2. **A recording-level recovery procedure with a compatibility-conditioned risk-gap guarantee.** We define the recording-level estimand apart from its window-level counterpart and prove a finite-sample risk-gap guarantee for the global empirical $K$-means solution under a fixed number of environments and a fixed feature transformation. The population alignment enters as an explicit compatibility premise, stated rather than derived. The global, finite-Lloyd, and blind variants are separated, and automatic $K$ selection, full-pool standardization, and finite-initialization Lloyd stay outside the unconditional guarantees.

3. **A pre-specified, unit-aware empirical protocol over three independent EEG datasets.** The independent statistical unit is the recording. We report where blind recovery holds and where it fails: source-associated structure is recoverable at a known $K$ and a balanced design, the blind selector under-counts, the unbalanced plug-in is weak, and within-source recovery is absent. The recovered object is a technical, dataset-level structure tied to acquisition and protocol, never a causal environment, and the negative findings appear as boundary checks.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the related work and positions the paper against it. Section 3 formalizes the problem, the two observation mechanisms, and the notation. Section 4 develops the identifiability boundary and Section 5 states the recovery theory. Sections 6 and 7 report the synthetic and real-EEG experiments, respectively. Section 8 discusses the limitations and concludes.
